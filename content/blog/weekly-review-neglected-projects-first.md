Here's how most weekly reviews go. You open your project list, start at the top, and spend twenty good minutes on the project you're excited about. Then you skim the rest, because it's Friday afternoon and you've already done the hard thinking. The project that quietly stopped moving three weeks ago gets a glance and a "next week".

Nothing in that ritual is wrong, exactly. It's just ordered by enthusiasm, and enthusiasm is the opposite of what a review is for.

So when we rebuilt the weekly plan in Exponential, we changed one thing that turned out to matter more than everything else: the order.

## Sort by drift, not by preference

In a workspace's weekly review, every project gets a health score. It starts at 100 and loses points for the usual signs of drift:

| Sign of drift | Points |
|---|---|
| No active next action | −30 |
| Nothing completed in the last 7 days | −25 |
| Overdue actions | −20 |
| No linked key result | −15 |
| No end date | −15 |
| Stuck or blocked | −10 |
| No description | −10 |

The lowest score goes first. The project with no next action, nothing finished this week and no key result tied to it is the first thing you look at, while you still have energy.

None of these signals is clever, and that's the point. Each one is something you'd spot yourself if you looked carefully. The score just makes sure you look at the right project first, instead of the one you like.

## Four steps, in order

The review across all your workspaces runs in four steps:

1. **Triage your workspaces.** Mark each one *in focus* or *dormant* for the week, and give the in-focus ones a theme. A side project can sit a week out without cluttering everything else.
2. **Check in on your key results.** Click a value, type the new number, move on. Progress and status update from the check-in.
3. **Re-rank your projects** into Top focus (five at most), Active and Backlog. This changes each project's real priority everywhere in the app, not just inside the review. If a project serves one of your focus key results but isn't in your top five, a banner offers to promote it.
4. **Go project by project.** Status, priority, the next action, and one reflection box: *What changed since last week? What's the unlock?*

Progress saves as you go, so closing the tab halfway through costs nothing.

## Make it end on time

A review that has no end turns into a planning session, and that turns into a reorganisation of your entire task system. Every productivity person has been there, usually on a Sunday.

So there are two modes, Quick Sweep for a light week and Full Review for a thorough one, and an optional timer: 10, 15 or 25 minutes, or none. Pick the shortest one that's honest about the week you had.

## Streaks, without the confetti

Finishing a review extends your streak, with milestones at 4, 12, 26 and 52 weeks. I'm wary of productivity apps congratulating people, but a visible streak does one useful thing: it turns skipping a week into a decision rather than something that just happened.

For teams, a finished review shows up in the workspace's activity, and the weekly team check-in can share a summary of what changed with your team or post it to Slack. The summary is a plain account of the changes, not a paragraph of AI prose about how productive everyone was.

## Try the order, even without the tool

You don't need Exponential to steal the idea. Next time you review your week, sort your projects by how neglected they are before you start: no next action, nothing finished, no end date. Then start at the top.
